**Abstract**

As digital services are growing quickly, it's more important than ever to understand the factors that impact consumer trust in digital environments. This study examines the link between consumer trust to adopt digital services, perceived system reliability, perceived data privacy and cybersecurity awareness. Using the lens of the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and Protection Motivation Theory (PMT), the research considers if these are factors affecting trust and if there are differences between age groups.
The research philosophy used was a positivist approach and a deductive approach was used. The study was carried out using a structured online questionnaire that was administered to 124 respondents who use digital services. The Study used descriptive statistics, reliability analysis, one way ANOVA, correlation analysis and multiple regression analysis with the help of python.
The results indicate medium level of cybersecurity awareness, perceived data privacy, system reliability as well as consumer trust in the respondents. No significant differences of consumer trust were found across the demographic groups. The results of the correlation analysis indicated that data privacy and system reliability were significantly positively related to the trust of the consumers and the cybersecurity awareness was not significantly related to the trust. Further results from the regression analysis showed that perceived data privacy and system reliability emerged as significant factors that had a significant impact on consumer trust, with system reliability having the greatest impact.
The findings advance the literature of business analytics by showing that when it comes to trust, it is not just about awareness, but also perceptions of privacy protection and reliable performance of digital services. The findings offer actionable insights for organisations aiming to bolster consumer confidence and promote sustainable use of digital services.


Key words: Cybersecurity Awareness, Consumer Trust, Digital Service Adoption, Data Privacy, System Reliability, Technology Acceptance Model, Protection Motivation Theory, Business Analytics.
